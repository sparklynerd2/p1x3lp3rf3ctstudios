Mega Game Break

Privacy Policy

Effective and last updated: November 21, 2026

This policy explains what information Mega Game Break handles, why, and the choices you have. Mega Game Break is made by P1x3l P3rf3ct Studios ("we", "us").

The short version

  • The app is free with ads, shown by Google AdMob. A one-time No ads purchase removes them.
  • It goes online to download new puzzles, and, only if you choose to, to put your best times on the leaderboards under a nickname.
  • Your puzzle progress is saved on your phone. If you choose to sign in with Google, a copy is kept with your account so it syncs between your phones.
  • No account is needed to play. Signing in with Google is optional and shares your Google email and name with us (never with other players). If you join the leaderboards, your nickname and best times are shared.
  • Ads are shown until you buy No ads.

1. Information we collect

Mega Game Break does not need an account to play, and has no analytics or crash-reporting tools. We receive information about you only if you choose to join the leaderboards or sign in with Google (see Leaderboards and Google sign-in below). Separately, the free version shows ads from Google, which collects some information from your device to show them (see Ads below); buying No ads stops this.

2. New puzzles from the internet

We add new jigsaw pictures, word searches and crosswords from time to time. To show them, the app downloads a list of new puzzles now and then, and downloads a picture the first time you open it. These come from our Google Firebase project. They are plain downloads: the app sends no account, name, identifier or information about your progress. As with any internet connection, Google sees your device's IP address to deliver the files. Downloaded puzzles are kept on your device so you can play them offline.

3. Leaderboards and Google sign-in

Leaderboards are optional. The first time you open one, the app asks whether you want to share your best times and, if so, which nickname to show. If you say no, nothing about you is sent, and you can still look at the leaderboards.

If you say yes, the app signs in to our Google Firebase project with an anonymous ID created for your installation (no email, phone number or other account details), and stores, for each puzzle you set a personal best on, your nickname, your best time (or, for The Traitor Word, your longest win streak) and when it was set. Other players can see your nickname and times on the leaderboards. Please do not use your real name or anything personal as your nickname.

In Records you can also, if you like, sign in with your Google account, so your progress and leaderboard entries follow you to other phones. Google then shares your Google account's email address, name and an account ID with our Firebase project, which keeps them with your anonymous ID to recognise you when you sign in again. While you are signed in, the app also keeps a copy of your progress (which puzzles you have solved, your best times, your Traitor Word stats and your nickname) with your account in our Firebase project, so it is the same on every phone you sign in on; only you can read it. Your account details are never shown to other players (only your nickname is), and are not used for anything else. To have your account and its progress deleted, email us (see Contact us). You can sign out in Records at any time.

You can change your nickname or stop sharing at any time in Records. To have your leaderboard entries deleted, email us (see Contact us) with your nickname; uninstalling the app also ends the anonymous ID, but entries already shared stay until deleted. We may remove entries with unsuitable nicknames or impossible times.

4. What stays on your device

So you can carry on where you left off, the app saves on your device which puzzles you have solved and your best time on each, any puzzle you are part-way through, your Traitor Word stats, the last pack or category you looked at, and whether you have bought No ads. Uninstalling the app, or clearing its storage in Android Settings, deletes all of this from the phone. If you are signed in with Google, the synced copy described above stays with your account until you ask us to delete it.

5. Device backup

If you have Android backup turned on, Android may include the app's saved progress in your device backup so it can be restored on a new phone. That backup is handled by Google under your Google account and its privacy policy; we cannot see it. You can turn backup off in Android Settings.

6. Links that open your browser

A few buttons open a web page in your browser: Photo source, shown when you finish a jigsaw made from a credited photograph, and the Privacy Policy and Terms of Service links on the home screen. Those pages are visited by your browser, not the app, and other websites are covered by their own privacy policies.

7. Device permissions

The app asks for internet access, which it uses to download new puzzles, for the leaderboards if you join, and for ads; the ads also use the advertising ID permission, and Google Play's billing permission is used for the No ads purchase. It has no access to your camera, microphone, location, contacts or files.

8. Ads

The free version shows ads from Google AdMob: a banner at the bottom of the puzzle lists, and now and then a full-screen ad between puzzles. To show and measure ads, and to limit fraud, Google collects information from your device, such as its advertising ID, IP address, and how you interact with ads. In the European Economic Area, the UK and Switzerland the app asks for your consent first, using Google's consent form, and you can show fewer personalised ads. You can also reset or delete your advertising ID in your phone's settings (Settings, Google, Ads, or Settings, Privacy, Ads). Ads are limited to ones suitable for general audiences. Google handles this information under its own policy: How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services. We do not receive information that identifies you.

9. Purchases

The app is free. A one-time No ads purchase removes all ads. It is handled by Google Play, which processes the payment; we never see your payment details. The app checks with Google Play whether your account owns No ads, so it comes back by itself on a new phone or after reinstalling.

10. Children

Mega Game Break is made for a general audience and is not directed at children under 13. Ads are limited to ones suitable for general audiences. Playing needs no personal information; the optional leaderboards share only a chosen nickname and best times, and we ask players not to use their real names. If you believe a child under 13 has signed in with Google or shared information with us, email us and we will delete it.

11. Changes to this policy

If we change this policy, we will update the date at the top of this page. For important changes we will also let you know in the app or on its Google Play listing before they take effect.
